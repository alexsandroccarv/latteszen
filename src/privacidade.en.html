About this document

lattesZen is free software (AGPLv3 license), maintained independently, to help public-service, teaching and research professionals organize their own Lattes Curriculum. This document explains, plainly, what happens to the data you enter while using the tool.

This text is informational and aims to faithfully reflect the technical behavior of lattesZen — since the code is open, you (or anyone) can verify every statement below directly in the project's repository.

1. How lattesZen works (no server)

lattesZen is an app that runs entirely in your browser (client-side). There is no server, backend, remote database or dedicated API that your curriculum information is sent to — there is also no sign-up, login or password of any kind.

  • The catalog index (the items you enter) and your preferences (theme, high contrast, font size) are saved in your browser's localStorage, tied to this site.
  • By default, if your browser supports it (Chrome/Edge) and you choose a folder in Settings, each item's files (PDF + metadata) and the curriculum's other folders (evidence, backup, export, publish) are read from and written directly to your computer, without going through any intermediary server.
  • Optionally, instead of a local folder, you can connect a Google Drive account (useful for accessing the same catalog from more than one computer) — in that case, the same files are written to a folder in your own Drive, straight from the browser to Google's API, without passing through a lattesZen server — see details in section 4.
This means that, without an action on your part (exporting, publishing, donating, or connecting Google Drive), no data from your curriculum leaves your device.

2. What data exists and where it lives

The data handled by lattesZen is exclusively what you type in or import yourself: identification, education, output, professional activities, projects, events, evidence (PDFs), and, if the module is enabled, functional data for the RSC-PCCTAE simulator (position, SIAPE ID, department, education level — a Brazilian federal civil-service scheme). None of this is collected by us — you enter it, and it stays with you.

3. What lattesZen does not do

  • Has no server, backend or database of its own that your data is sent to.
  • Doesn't require an account, login, password or email to use.
  • Doesn't use advertising tools or social-network pixels (Meta Pixel and similar). The site uses Google Analytics, with your consent, for visit metrics — see details in section 4, below.
  • Doesn't sell, rent, share or transfer data to third parties on its own initiative. The exceptions are always by your explicit choice: Google Analytics (browsing metrics, with consent) and Google Drive (if you opt for that storage mode instead of the local folder) — both detailed in section 4.
  • Doesn't automatically send anything to the Lattes Platform or any other service — every export/publish is an explicit action on your part.

4. Third-party resources loaded by the page

To display the interface, the browser fetches a few public libraries directly from their original providers:

  • Tailwind CSS (cdn.tailwindcss.com) — visual styling.
  • Font Awesome (cdnjs.cloudflare.com) — icons.
  • Rawline font and Gov.br logo (cdngovbr-ds.estaleiro.serpro.gov.br) — the Brazilian Federal Government visual identity standard.

These requests follow normal web behavior: the providers may log standard access metadata (such as IP address and browser used), per their own policies — lattesZen sends them no additional data, and has no control over those logs. If any of these resources fails to load (slow network, firewall block), the app keeps working with a simpler style.

Google Drive (optional storage)

In Settings, you can choose to connect a Google Drive account as an alternative to a local folder — useful for accessing the same catalog from more than one computer. This feature is off by default: it's only enabled if you click "Connect to Google Drive" yourself and authorize access through Google's own consent screen.

Authentication uses Google Identity Services — with no lattesZen server involved, authorization happens directly between your browser and Google. Access uses the drive.file scope: lattesZen only sees files it created itself in your Drive, or ones you explicitly pick through Google's file picker — never the rest of your Drive.

Once connected, your catalog's files (evidence PDFs, each item's metadata, backups) are written to and read straight from your browser to the Google Drive API, under Google's privacy policy — your curriculum's content then also exists on Google, in addition to your browser/computer. You can disconnect at any time in Settings (which doesn't delete files already written to Drive) and go back to local mode.

Google Analytics (GA4)

Unlike the resources above (which only load visual libraries), Google Analytics collects and logs browsing data: pages visited, session duration, device/browser type, IP address (used to estimate approximate region), and referring site — never your curriculum's content (cataloged items, PDFs, RSC data), which stays exclusively in your browser/computer, as described in sections 1 and 2. Before any collection happens, a cookie notice blocks use of the app until you click "Accept" — without that decision, no request leaves your browser toward Google (there is no "decline and continue" option: either you accept, or you don't use that instance). Once accepted, data starts going to Google, under Google's privacy policy, and is available in aggregate form to whoever maintains lattesZen. Google Analytics uses cookies (e.g. _ga) to tell access sessions apart; your acceptance decision is also saved in your browser — to revisit it, just clear this site's browsing data, and the notice will block the app again until a new decision on your next visit.

Want maximum privacy? Since it's free, open-source software, you don't need to trust any third-party-hosted instance (including the official one): download or clone the repository and run lattesZen fully locally, on your own computer. Running it that way, clear the analyticsId value in src/js/config.js before running it — without an ID configured, Google Analytics is disabled and no cookie notice appears, guaranteeing that no browsing data leaves your browser. Installed as an app (PWA), lattesZen also keeps working normally day to day without an internet connection.

5. Actions that leave lattesZen

Some features, by nature, take information outside the app — always on your own initiative:

  • Publish Online: generates an HTML page (and the folder ready to host) on your computer; if you choose to publish it on some hosting service, that data's public exposure is then governed by that service's privacy policy.
  • Buy me a coffee: the Pix (Brazil's instant-payment system) QR code is generated by querying the external service api.qrserver.com, which only receives the Pix "copy and paste" code (the donation recipient's data, not yours) to draw the image. That page also carries partner/affiliate links (e.g. Preply, Airbnb) — clicking one takes you outside lattesZen and subjects you to each destination site's own privacy policy.

6. Legal basis (LGPD)

Your curriculum's content (cataloged items, PDFs, RSC data) is processed entirely on your device, with no centralized collection or processing by the developer — this use falls under data processing carried out by a natural person for exclusively personal purposes (art. 4, §1, of the LGPD), outside the scope of the processing obligations the law imposes on controllers of third-party data.

The site's access metrics (Google Analytics, section 4), on the other hand, are a separate processing activity, the responsibility of whoever maintains lattesZen, based on legitimate interest (art. 7, IX, of the LGPD) in understanding how the tool is used in order to improve it — limited to browsing data, never your curriculum's content.

When you choose to engage in activities involving third-party services — importing from the Lattes Platform, hosting the public page, donating via Pix, or using a partner link — the privacy policy and legal basis of each service used applies, not this document's.

7. Your rights as a data subject

Article 18 of the LGPD guarantees rights such as access, correction, deletion and portability of data. Since everything lives in your own browser/computer, you already exercise these rights directly, without needing to request anything from anyone:

  • Access and correction: view and edit any item at any time on the Catalog tab.
  • Deletion: use Clear catalog (Settings) and/or delete the files in the chosen folder through your operating system.
  • Portability: export the backup (JSON) whenever you want to take your data elsewhere.
  • Revocation: simply stop using the app and delete the local data — there's no account to cancel and no server to request deletion from.

8. Children and teenagers

lattesZen is a tool intended for organizing the Lattes Curriculum by public-service, teaching and research professionals, and is not directed at children or teenagers.
